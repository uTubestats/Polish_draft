Writing tips

How to write a professional email

Lead with the point. Your first sentence should say why you are writing. Busy readers decide in seconds whether to keep reading.

Write a specific subject line. “Question about Friday’s invoice” beats “Hello”. Include a date or action when there is one.

One email, one purpose. If you need three unrelated things, number them or send separate emails.

Say what you need and by when. “Could you confirm by Thursday 3 pm?” is easier to act on than “Let me know soon.”

Close simply. Thank them once, then sign off with your name. Before sending, read it aloud and check names, dates and attachments.

How to make an email sound more polite

Politeness is mostly framing, not extra words. Replace commands with requests (“Could you send…” instead of “Send…”), acknowledge the reader’s effort, and explain the reason behind a request.

Blunt: “You didn’t send the report.”
Better: “I haven’t received the report yet. Could you let me know when to expect it?”

Avoid piling up apologies or softeners such as “just” and “sorry to bother you”, which can sound unsure. One clear, courteous sentence works better.

How to write a professional follow-up

Wait a reasonable time (often 2 to 5 working days), then reply to your original thread so the context is attached. Open with a short reminder, restate the one action you need, and offer an easy next step.

“Hi Sam, following up on my note from Monday about the Q3 schedule. Could you confirm which of the two dates suits you? Happy to adjust if neither works.”

Keep the tone neutral. Avoid “as per my last email”, which reads as irritation.
